Minor changes

PDFs with printed line numbers

#PRNUM

On a guideline that numbers every line in its left margin, the layout analysis took most pages for a table of contents: a column of numbers beside lines of text looks like one. The PDF reader drops a table of contents, so most of such a document's text never reached the target, with only a line in the report ("contents and lists on pages …") to show it. On the ICH Q1 draft guideline, 89 of 108 pages were dropped this way, about three quarters of its text. The layout analysis is now given those pages without their margin of line numbers, so it mostly sees ordinary pages of headings and paragraphs. Where it still takes a page for contents, the reader checks that the region reads as contents, with titles and page numbers, before dropping it; one that doesn't is kept as paragraphs, and its pages are listed in the report's first line. On the ICH draft, all but the three real contents pages are now delivered. On those 26 pages headings come out as plain paragraphs: the PDF has no bookmarks to find them by.

Line numbers were also left in on pages that a table or a figure fills, where too few lines are numbered for the page to show it alone. Once a document has shown that it numbers its lines, those pages lose the numbers that stand in the same column and carry on the count from the pages around them. A figure no longer shows the numbers beside it either: it is cropped from where the text starts.

Docling still takes a few tables drawn with heavy rules for pictures. Those are delivered as images, with their text after them; that is not changed here.
